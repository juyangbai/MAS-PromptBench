When Does Prompt Optimization Improve Multi-Agent LLM Systems?

Prompt-optimization gains using a state-of-the-art optimizer MIPRO in single-agent and multi-agent settings. While MIPRO consistently improves single-agent performance across all five diverse tasks, its natural multi-agent extension yields highly variable effects across tasks and workflow topologies, ranging from large gains to severe performance drops.

How much can prompt optimization help in MAS,
and how does its effect vary across configurations?

Contributions

1

MAS-PromptBench: a benchmark for MAS prompt optimization

A comprehensive benchmark for evaluating system-prompt optimizers for MAS. It spans diverse MAS configurations across three task domains (reasoning, coding, and tool calling), five workflow topologies (both existing and newly constructed systems), communication protocols from free-form to highly structured coordination, and varying team sizes. It also evaluates eight prompt optimizers — two that naturally extend state-of-the-art single-agent methods and six designed specifically for MAS — providing a foundation for proposing, analyzing, and comparing system-prompt optimization algorithms under controlled MAS configurations.

2

Prompt optimization gains and failures for MAS

Systematically evaluating the gains over default system prompts achieved by four representative optimizers: MAS-GEPA and MAS-MIPRO, built on GEPA and MIPRO, and the MAS-specific MAPRO and MASPO. The results highlight the promise of prompt optimization for MAS: improvements reach up to +56.0 points. Yet they also reveal the need for principled algorithms tailored to multi-agent settings, as performance can drop by as much as −12.0 points for certain configurations.

3

Insights into when prompt optimization works for MAS

Prompt optimization shows greater potential when tasks have explicit, controllable, and verifiable agent-local behaviors, and when communication protocols adopt a free form that leaves agent messages open to revision; it also needs to be workflow-topology-aware. Optimization becomes harder as team size grows — confirming the challenges of scaling MAS prompt optimization and motivating principled, more scalable, and robust algorithms.

Overview of benchmark MAS-PromptBench. Given an input task, a multi-agent system produces a final solution through interactions among LLM-based agents. Using state-of-the-art prompt optimizers, MAS-PromptBench measures prompt-optimization gains across four axes: task distribution, workflow topology, communication protocol, and team size.

We model a MAS as a tuple $\mathcal{M} = (\mathcal{A}, G, P)$: an ordered collection of $n$ agents $\mathcal{A}$, an inter-agent coordination workflow $G$, and a communication protocol $P$. Each agent $A_i = (\theta_i, \pi_i)$ pairs a frozen LLM with parameters $\theta_i$ and a learnable system prompt $\pi_i$. System-prompt optimization maximizes the expected task score $\mathbb{E}_{(x,e)\sim\mathcal{T}}[\mu(\mathcal{M}(x;\pi), e)]$ over the joint prompts $\pi$ under a budget of $B$ MAS rollouts, where $e$ is the evaluation reference (e.g., a ground-truth answer or unit tests). Fixing an optimizer and the base model, for a configuration $(\mathcal{T}, G, n, P)$ we measure the prompt-optimization gain $\Delta$ as the difference between the optimized ($\pi^\star$) and initial ($\pi^0$) system scores:

$$\Delta(\mathcal{T}, G, n, P) := \mathbb{E}_{(x,e)\sim\mathcal{T}}\big[\,\mu(\mathcal{M}(x;\pi^\star), e) - \mu(\mathcal{M}(x;\pi^0), e)\,\big]$$

The five workflow topologies in MAS-PromptBench. Arrows indicate message flow.

Single. A single LLM serves as the baseline.
Independent. $n$ agents solve the task in parallel without inter-agent communication, and their outputs are aggregated by majority vote.
Sequential. Agents form a directed chain $A_1 \to A_2 \to \cdots \to A_n$ with no backward edges; each agent receives the previous agent's output as input toward the final answer.
Centralized. A coordinator dispatches subtasks to sub-agents $A_1, \ldots, A_n$, collects their outputs, and aggregates them into the final answer; sub-agents do not communicate with one another.
Decentralized. All $n$ agents communicate over a fully connected graph and exchange messages for a fixed number of rounds; final-round outputs are aggregated by majority vote for question answering or best-of-$N$ test-pass for coding.

Findings

When does a better local prompt become a better system? We vary one MAS factor at a time and measure the prompt-optimization gains of MAS-GEPA, MAS-MIPRO, MAPRO, and MASPO.

Factor 1 · Task

Explicit, verifiable tasks gain more

System-prompt optimization is broadly promising: averaged over workflows, the best optimizer per workflow improves all nine tasks, with the largest average gain of +10.4 points on SWE-Bench. Individual configurations gain even more — with MASPO, Decentralized improves by +22.3 on API-Bank and +20.5 on ToolHop, and Centralized by +20.0 on SWE-Bench. Gains are larger for coding and tool-calling (average best-optimizer gains of +5.9 and +5.5 points) than for reasoning (+2.6), where the peak is only +11.3 on Independent HotpotQA.

Blue indicates improvement, orange indicates regression, and gray indicates no change. Avg. Max: mean best-optimizer gain over topologies. MAPRO and MASPO are not run on Single.

We hypothesize that the difference comes from how explicitly a task decomposes into controllable local behaviors. Coding tasks expose verifiable artifacts that compilation and tests can check, and tool-calling tasks offer structured interfaces — explicit function names and output formats — that system prompts can directly shape, so local prompt improvements survive downstream coordination. Reasoning tasks rely on correlated logical steps with implicit intermediate feedback, so local improvements are often discarded, overwritten, or amplified before reaching the final answer.

Takeaway. Prompt optimization shows greater potential on tasks with explicit, controllable, and verifiable agent-local behaviors, such as coding and tool-calling, than on reasoning tasks.
Factor 2 · Workflow Topology

Multi-agent systems need topology-aware optimizers

Prompt-optimization gain Δ (pp) of each optimizer across workflow topologies, averaged over nine tasks. MAPRO and MASPO are not run on Single.

No evaluated optimizer transfers uniformly across topologies. MAS-GEPA gains +6.0 on Sequential but only +0.2 on Centralized, and MAS-MIPRO gains +7.5 on Sequential but only +1.2 on Independent and Decentralized. The best optimizer also changes with the topology: MAS-GEPA leads on Independent, MAS-MIPRO on Sequential and Centralized, and MASPO on Decentralized. Sequential yields the largest gains (+4.8 on average across the four optimizers), suggesting that an improved prompt early in the chain propagates to every downstream agent; Independent and Decentralized yield the smallest (+1.2 and +1.0), as aggregating parallel agent outputs may absorb much of the effect of prompt revisions.

Takeaway. Multi-agent systems need topology-aware prompt optimizers.
Factor 3 · Communication Protocol

Freeform messages leave more room to optimize

We compare three protocols with increasing structure: Freeform natural-language messages with no required fields; Semi-structured messages with prescribed slots (status, evidence, confidence, next step) filled in natural language; and Structured JSON-style reports whose slot values follow a constrained, finite set of options.

Prompt-optimization gain Δ (pp) by communication protocol, averaged over HotpotQA, LiveCodeBench, and BFCL and four MAS topologies. Freeform yields the largest gain for every optimizer.

Averaged over MAS-GEPA, MAS-MIPRO, and MASPO, the gain is +4.9 points under Freeform messages, compared with +2.4 under Semi-structured and +3.0 under Structured messages. The advantage is largest for MAS-MIPRO, where Freeform (+5.3) is about twice Semi-structured (+2.7) and Structured (+2.6). Freeform messages let the optimizer revise both the content and the form of what each agent passes downstream, whereas a fixed template already determines much of each message, leaving less for prompt optimization to change.

Full results by communication protocol

Takeaway. Freeform communication leaves agent messages open to revision, giving MAS prompt optimization more room to improve than protocols that fix message structure in advance.
Factor 4 · Team Size

Larger teams make optimization harder

Prompt-optimization gain Δ (pp) across team sizes, averaged over HotpotQA, LiveCodeBench, and BFCL and four MAS topologies. The dashed line is the mean across the three optimizers.

As teams grow ($n \in \{4, 8, 10\}$), gains generally shrink: averaged over MAS-GEPA, MAS-MIPRO, and MASPO, they fall from +4.9 points at $n{=}4$ to +2.6 at $n{=}8$ and remain at +2.6 at $n{=}10$. Larger teams enable finer-grained specialization but also add handoffs and intermediate states, making local improvements harder to preserve. The effect is clearest for MAS-GEPA (+5.1 → +2.4 → +1.7), while MAS-MIPRO retains most of its gain (+5.3, +4.3, +4.0), so the effect of team size also depends on the optimizer.

Full results by team size

Prompt-optimization gains across team sizes on HotpotQA, LiveCodeBench, and BFCL. Each cell reports baseline / optimized performance, followed by the signed change $\Delta$ in percentage points with its standard deviation across three optimizer seeds.

Takeaway. Larger team size increases the challenge of prompt optimization for MAS, as local agent improvements may fail to produce system-level gains.
Ablation · Optimizers & Task Models

The topology effect holds across optimizers and task models

We repeat the workflow-topology study on HotpotQA, LiveCodeBench, and BFCL with four more MAS prompt optimizers: HiveMind, MAMUT-GEPA, MASPOB, and TRUCE. The Sequential topology yields the largest gain for six of the eight optimizers (+6.7 points on average), whereas Independent and Decentralized yield the smallest (+1.4 and +1.2). The best optimizer again changes with the topology: MAS-GEPA leads on Single and Independent, MAS-MIPRO on Sequential and Centralized, and TRUCE on Decentralized.
